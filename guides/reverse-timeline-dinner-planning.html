The Reverse Timeline: How to Get Every Course on the Table at Once

Professional kitchens plan backwards from service time. Here's how to do the same at home so every course arrives hot and you stay calm.

By the Gastrosex.com editors. Updated November 2026.

Most home-cooking stress comes from timing, not technique. Everything is fine until three things need your attention at once. Restaurants solve this with a simple idea: start from the moment you want to eat and work backwards.

Step 1: Pick your serving time

Choose when the main course should hit the table, say 8:00 pm. Everything else is scheduled relative to that moment.

Step 2: List every task with its duration

Go through each recipe and write down every task with its time, including resting and oven preheating. Separate "active" time (you're busy) from "passive" time (the oven or the fridge is busy).

Step 3: Work backwards

For each dish, subtract its total time from the serving time to find its start time. For example, for Crispy Smashed Potatoes with Garlic & Herbs at 8:00 pm:

  • 7:58 Brush with garlic butter
  • 7:30 Into the oven (28 minutes)
  • 7:22 Drain, steam-dry, smash
  • 7:02 Start boiling (18 minutes)
  • 6:50 Turn the oven on

Step 4: Find the collisions

Put all dishes on one timeline and look for moments where two active tasks overlap. Fix collisions by moving one task earlier (most sauces and salads tolerate it) or by choosing a make-ahead dish. This is why we recommend at least one course that is finished before guests arrive, like Crème Brûlée for Two.

Step 5: Mise en place

Mise en place means "everything in its place." Before any heat goes on, measure, chop and arrange every ingredient in small bowls in the order you'll use them. It makes even a complex dish like Creamy Mushroom Risotto feel calm.

Step 6: Build in a buffer

Add 10 minutes of slack before your guest arrives. Use it to tidy the kitchen, change your shirt or light the candles.
