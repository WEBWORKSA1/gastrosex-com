The 12 Kitchen Tools That Make You Look Like a Pro

The short list of equipment that actually improves your cooking, from the thermometer to the fish spatula, and what you can skip.

By the Gastrosex.com editors. Updated November 2026.

You don't need a professional kitchen to cook to impress, but a few well-chosen tools make a big difference to your results and your confidence. Here is our shortlist, in order of impact.

  1. Instant-read thermometer. The single biggest upgrade for meat and fish. It removes the guesswork that ruins steaks. Essential for Pan-Seared Ribeye with Red Wine Pan Sauce.
  2. A sharp 20 cm (8 in) chef's knife. A sharp knife is safer than a blunt one because it doesn't slip. Have it professionally sharpened once or twice a year.
  3. A large, stable cutting board. Wood or composite, at least 40 × 30 cm. Put a damp towel under it so it doesn't slide.
  4. A 30 cm (12 in) cast-iron or heavy stainless skillet. For proper searing. Non-stick pans don't get hot enough for a real crust.
  5. A rimmed sheet pan. For roasting, resting and organizing your prep.
  6. A fish spatula. Thin, flexible and angled; useful for far more than fish.
  7. Tongs. An extension of your hand for flipping, turning and plating pasta.
  8. A microplane grater. For citrus zest, garlic, Parmesan and chocolate.
  9. Small prep bowls. They make mise en place possible. See The Reverse Timeline: How to Get Every Course on the Table at Once.
  10. Two ramekins. For Molten Chocolate Lava Cakes for Two and Crème Brûlée for Two.
  11. A pepper mill. Freshly ground pepper tastes completely different from pre-ground.
  12. A small kitchen torch. Optional, but there's no better finale than torching crème brûlée at the table.

What you can skip

Garlic presses (a knife or microplane is better), single-use gadgets, and large knife blocks full of knives you'll never use. Spend the money on one excellent knife.
